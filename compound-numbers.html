Compound Numbers · Reference

In Chaldean numerology, compound numbers (10–52) carry distinct vibrational meanings beyond their single-digit root. Each is classified as Auspicious or Inauspicious. For names producing numbers above 52, a chain-reduction method determines the verdict.

What is a Compound Number?
When the Chaldean sum of your name exceeds 9, it becomes a compound number. Unlike in Pythagorean numerology where only the reduced single digit matters, Chaldean numerology treats each two-digit (or higher) sum as having its own energy.

Numbers 1–9 are single digits — no compound reading applies.
Numbers 10–52 each have a defined auspicious or inauspicious quality.
Numbers above 52 are handled via the chain-reduction algorithm below.
How to Calculate for Numbers Above 52
1
Subtract 9 repeatedly from the compound number until you reach a value ≤ 52. This is your entry point into the known compound range.
2
Build a chain: starting from the entry point, keep subtracting 9 until you go below 10. Collect every value ≥ 10 in this chain.
3
Check each chain number against the auspicious set. Count how many are auspicious vs inauspicious.
4
Majority vote: if auspicious count ≥ half the chain length, the overall verdict is Auspicious; otherwise Inauspicious.
Example — Name sum = 61
Step 1: 61 − 9 = 52  (≤ 52, entry point reached)
Step 2: Chain from 52 → 52, 43, 34, 25, 16  (stop before 10)
Step 3: Check each —
52 ✓ → 43 ✗ → 34 ✓ → 25 ✓ → 16 ✗
Step 4: 3 auspicious out of 5 → majority → ✓ Auspicious (3/5)
Example — Name sum = 74
Step 1: 74 − 9 = 65, 65 − 9 = 56, 56 − 9 = 47  (≤ 52, entry point)
Step 2: Chain from 47 → 47, 38, 29, 20, 11
Step 3: Check each —
47 ✗ → 38 ✗ → 29 ✗ → 20 ✓ → 11 ✗
Step 4: 2 auspicious out of 5 → minority → ✗ Inauspicious (2/5)
